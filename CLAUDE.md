## Design Context

### Users
ExMarket serves three primary user groups with equal priority:

- AI prompt creators who publish, price, manage, and earn from premium prompts and agent workflows.
- Prompt buyers who browse, compare, unlock, and revisit purchased prompts with confidence.
- Web3/API builders who care about protocol credibility, access control, on-chain transparency, and reliable API-style usage.

The interface must support quick scanning for marketplace discovery, clear confidence cues for wallet/payment flows, and enough technical clarity for users who want to understand how Aptos, Shelby, and access gating fit together.

### Brand Personality
ExMarket should feel trustworthy, premium, and technical.

The voice should be confident and precise, without hype. It can have personality, but it should never feel gimmicky, juvenile, or like a generic AI SaaS template. Trust comes from clarity, visible system state, honest error handling, and consistent interaction patterns.

### Aesthetic Direction
The visual direction is neo-brutalism plus glassmorphism:

- Keep strong structure: heavy borders, offset shadows, chunky controls, crisp panels, and confident hierarchy.
- Keep the glass layer: translucent surfaces, blur, subtle grain/noise, and frosted depth where it supports readability.
- Use a dark-first interface with muted retro color accents.
- Avoid colors that are too bright, neon, garish, or visually loud.
- Avoid generic AI aesthetics: purple-blue gradients, vague glowing SaaS dashboards, stock cyber visuals, and decoration that does not clarify the product.
- The UI should feel premium and technical first, expressive second.

### Design Principles

1. Make trust visible.
   Wallet state, loading, access, payments, errors, and on-chain actions should be explicit, calm, and easy to verify.

2. Balance creator, buyer, and builder needs.
   Marketplace browsing, creator workflows, and technical/protocol details must all feel first-class.

3. Use neo-brutal glass with restraint.
   Heavy structure and frosted surfaces are the signature, but color, motion, and decoration should stay controlled.

4. Prefer product clarity over spectacle.
   Every panel, badge, animation, and accent should help users scan, compare, publish, unlock, or understand state.

5. Do not become generic.
   Future design work should preserve ExMarket's distinctive retro technical identity while improving accessibility, responsiveness, and polish.
